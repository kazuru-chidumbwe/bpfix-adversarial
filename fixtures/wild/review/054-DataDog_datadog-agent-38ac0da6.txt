perf(dyninst): free up BPF stack on the swiss-map AES chain and guard it with a test (#58160)

### What does this PR do?

Two stack-budget changes to the dyninst eBPF program:

- The swiss-map AES helpers spelled MixColumns out one column at a time, which
  made clang keep all 16 tmp bytes live and spill 216/208 bytes of frame. Loop
  over the columns instead; frames drop to 72 and 32 bytes.
- Add `pkg/dyninst/ebpf/stackbudget`, which reads a compiled eBPF object,
  rebuilds the call graph, and reports the worst combined stack. Both
  architectures are built and asserted in a unit test.

### Motivation

`probe_run_with_cookie -> sm_loop -> sm_swiss_map_aes{e,enc}` sits at exactly 512
bytes on arm64, the verifier's limit, so any new work anywhere on that chain
fails to load. Today you only find that out from a KMT job printing "combined
stack size of N calls is M. Too large", and because the limit is
architecture-sensitive, x86 CI never sees it.

### Describe how you validated your changes

The re-roll is bit-identical to the unrolled form over 3M pseudorandom (tmp, rk)
vectors plus single-byte edge patterns. The analyzer was checked against
known-good and known-bad objects: it reproduces the 512-byte chain on the
current tree and the rejected 544-byte chain reported by CI, frame count
included.

### Additional Notes

Split out of #56772, which needs the headroom. That PR is stacked on this one.
