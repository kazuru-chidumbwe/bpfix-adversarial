* feat(ebpf): IPv6 in virtual-ip mode: AAAA answers, connect6 and IPv6 TLS patching (--host-resolution=virtual-ip)

Fifth step of #327. Until now no TLS record sent over IPv6 was patched:
tc_egress_patch parsed IPv4 only and the tcp_sendmsg kprobe keyed
tc_pending on skc_daddr. In virtual-ip mode an AAAA answer also reached
the pod unchanged, so an app preferring IPv6 got no host-restricted
secret.

- --virtual-ip-range takes an IPv4 range and an optional IPv6 one,
  comma-separated; the default adds 2001:2::/48 (RFC 5180, the IPv6
  benchmarking range). planVirtualIPRanges plans each family with
  planVirtualIPs, half of vip_ip_host each; freeVirtualIP6 hashes into the
  low 64 bits. Without an IPv6 range, IPv6 reservations are released and
  AAAA answers pass unchanged.
- tc_dns_rewrite reads the question type and rewrites A records to the
  fake IPv4, AAAA records to the fake IPv6, over IPv4 or IPv6 (UDP right
  after the fixed header). vip_upstream slots hold 16-byte addresses; an
  empty slot has expires_at 0. Pods are bound by their IPv6 addresses too.
- vip_connect6 / vip_getpeername6 handle native IPv6 as well as the
  v4-mapped form; vip_redirect works on 16-byte addresses and refuses an
  upstream of the other family.
- The kprobe keys tc_pending on sock_dst_addr (IPv6, or IPv4-mapped),
  which socket_filter_patches checks a secret's IP against;
  tc_egress_patch parses IPv6/TCP and keys on bpf_sock.dst_ip6. Outside
  virtual-ip mode an IPv6 socket's patches are refused (sock_ipv6_deny),
  as tc never patched them: DNS snooping attributes IPv4 only.
- The startup overlap check reads the node's ipv6_route too.
- The address tests OR their bytes instead of exiting early: with clang
  19 (the Dockerfile's) the early exits took vip_connect6 past the
  verifier's 1M instructions; it now takes 3.6K.

TestTCEgressPatch (new) runs tc_egress_patch and tc_ghash_update on a real
TLS 1.3 AES-GCM record over IPv4 and IPv6 and checks it decrypts to the
real value; TestDNSRewrite and TestVirtualIPConnectRedirect cover AAAA,
DNS over IPv6 and native IPv6 connects.

* test(e2e): IPv6 virtual IPs on a dual-stack virtual-ip leg

TestVirtualIPv6 gives the raw TLS echo server an IPv6-only Service: a pod
with a secret for its name resolves it to the name's fake IPv6, connects
over IPv6 (getpeername reports the fake IPv6) and gets the secret
rewritten; a secret for the Service's IPv6 address is rewritten through
the fake IPv6; a direct connection to the address gets no secret
restricted to the name. The echo server listens on both families.

The virtual-ip CI leg runs k3s dual-stack (a ULA node address, fd42::/56
pods, fd43::/112 services); other Services stay IPv4. The e2e helpers
read both ranges of --virtual-ip-range and match a host's log lines by
family.

* fix(controller): trust every ClusterIP of kube-dns

A dual-stack kube-dns has an IPv4 and an IPv6 ClusterIP, and the kubelet
gives pods both as nameservers. musl's resolver queries them in parallel
and takes the first answer, but only Spec.ClusterIP (the primary family)
was trusted: tc_dns_rewrite left about half the answers unchanged, and an
Alpine curl connected to the real address and got no host-restricted
secret (TestCipherSuites/4hop on the dual-stack virtual-ip leg, with
main's image too). discoverTrustedDNSServers now trusts every ClusterIP.
